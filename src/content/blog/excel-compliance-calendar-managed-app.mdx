I recently had a request from a customer on behalf of one of their end users. The team has been running all of their quarterly and annual compliance reports out of an Excel spreadsheet that someone had built hears ago and they were having to continually update by hand. What a unique scenario, right?!

Knowing how powerful managed runtime applications are in Cowork and Copilot Studio, I decided to paste their question into Copilot Studio's app builder feature which is currently in preview, and see what it would come up with just from this very basic amount of input. Not only that, it seemed like a good way to get additional real-world Copilot Credit app building cost examples..

## The question, almost verbatim

The user wanted a better report and compliance calendar with task tracking. They had already researched it and found one common option: use Power Automate to keep Planner and Outlook in sync. They asked whether that was the best approach, how I'd recommend setting it up, and whether there was an even better way to solve the challenge.

I skipped their opening sentence and their last paragraph, the part asking for my recommendation, and pasted the rest into the app builder in Copilot Studio, word-for-word from the end-user's e-mail:

> We have several quarterly and annual reports that are due throughout the year and are the responsibility of different teams. Right now, we track everything in an Excel spreadsheet, but it requires constant manual updates.
>
> Our goal is to have report due dates visible and connected across Microsoft Teams Planner, Outlook Calendar, and SharePoint, while also being able to track ownership, status, and completion. I have spent time researching different options and trying to determine the best way to make these systems work together. I know a group calendar can be displayed in SharePoint, but that alone does not provide the tracking features we need.

That's the whole prompt. No design, no field list, no sample data provided.

## 2:30 pm paste to 2:55 pm v1.0 app testing

I pasted the prompt at 2:30 pm. By 2:55 pm I had an app I could click through, make changes, add new projects, etc. That's twenty-five minutes from paste to a working app.

It asked me for the SharePoint site I wanted it on, and if there was an existing SharePoint list or I wanted a new one. I opted for the new one and it built out a new list and all of the columns for me.

The first "front and center" view in the application is a clear project timeline. Each row is a team and each column is a month, grouped by quarter, with a marker for today. Every report is a pill on its team's row with a status icon, and the header keeps a running count: 11 open, 1 overdue.

## Connecting it to Planner and SharePoint

Later that evening I connected the app to Planner and SharePoint as the customer requested. The Planner plan is called Compliance Reports, with one bucket per team and one task per report. Each task carries its steps as a checklist and its due date.

When I say I connected it to Planner and Sharepoint, I actually just asked my agent to do this, and it gave me the steps to build the flow for that background sync process.

## So, is Power Automate the right answer?

It's a reasonable one. Connecting Planner and Outlook with Power Automate is a well-worn pattern, and it's what the customer's research had turned up. I'm not going to tell them it's wrong.

What this experiment gave me was the other half of the answer: what the team would actually look at every day. A flow keeps two systems in step, but someone still has to decide where owners, statuses, and due dates live. In 25 minutes the builder made that decision visible, with all of it on one screen.

Three things I'd take from it:

- **Paste the real request.** The customer's own words named the fields that mattered: ownership, status, completion, and due dates. I didn't need to translate them into a spec.
- **Test it like a user.** The Owner text box looked fine until I tried to use it. A short follow-up fixed it, but only because I clicked through the app.
- **Look at every system it touches.** The duplicates in Planner only showed up because I opened the board instead of trusting the app.

If your team has a spreadsheet that runs a process, try this with your own version of the question. Paste what you'd tell a colleague, publish to a test environment, and click through it before you decide what to build for real.
